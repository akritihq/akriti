## Review pass — nine defects, uncommitted

A read of `adapters.py` against §11 after the fact. Every one was reproduced
before it was fixed, and the eighteen new tests were written first: seventeen
failed against the committed code, and the two written after the fix were
mutation-checked separately (below).

### Behaviour

1. **`strip_padding=True` deleted rows that should raise.** `trivial = births
   == deaths` is true of `(inf, inf)`, which is an I4 violation, not giotto's
   padding — so a bad row was erased and counted in `padding_removed` under
   `True` while raising under `None`. §3.1 has one answer for a violation.
   The mask now requires the coordinates be finite.
2. **The clamp warning quoted the wrong number.** It reported the largest gap
   in the array, so an unrepaired violation appeared inside a sentence calling
   it floating-point noise. `_clamp_i6` now reports a maximum over the
   repaired rows, and the warning follows construction, so a call that raises
   warns about nothing at all.
3. **`dim=` bypassed the guard `_as_dims` exists to be.** `int(1.5)` is 1;
   `dim="2"` and `dim=True` were accepted too. `_as_degree` requires a
   `numbers.Integral` and rejects `bool`. Negative degrees stay with core's
   I3, which already names the value.

### Diagnostics

4. **Warnings pointed into `adapters.py`.** `stacklevel=3` from inside
   `_clamp_i6` named a library line the caller cannot act on. Warning is now
   issued one frame from the adapter, at the caller's line.
5. **`str` and `bytes` are `Sequence`s**, so they passed the type gate on
   three adapters and failed later with an exception about a character.
   `_is_row_sequence`.
6. **Malformed GUDHI rows** raised `too many values to unpack (expected 2)`.
   `_columns_from_pairs` unpacks per row and names the index.
7. **A degree list spanning two namespaces** raised something opaque from the
   backend. `core.py` guards exactly this across a batch and says why; the
   same check now runs within one diagram.
8. **`ripser({"dgms": 5})`** raised `'int' object is not subscriptable`. The
   key's presence and the key's shape are two facts; both are checked.

### Consistency

9. **Clamp warnings fired once per sample**, so a 500-sample batch emitted 500
   of them — the count a property of batch shape rather than of data, which is
   the argument §11.1 already settles for padding (D8). `_warn_clamped` is
   separate from `_clamp_i6`, and `from_giotto` sums across the batch and
   warns once.

Also: `_columns_from_table` was typed `tuple[Array, ...]` where it returns
three; `from_persim` annotated away the input its own `isinstance` check
rejects; `_source_dtype(arr)` was recomputed per sample from one array.

**Mutation checks.** Two tests were written after their fix, so both were run
against a deliberately broken `adapters.py`. Warning before validation instead
of after is caught by two tests. Reporting the maximum gap over all rows
rather than the repaired ones is **not** caught, and cannot be: in any call
that returns, every positive gap was either repaired or raised through I6, so
the two agree on every observable output. The masking is defensive, the
ordering is what carries the behaviour, and `_Clamped`'s docstring says so
rather than implying the test suite covers it.

## External review pass — one defect, uncommitted

An outside review against the **revised** RFC (`22da8d8`, on
`origin/rfc-0001-persistence-diagram-interchange-draft-revisions`, unmerged and
carrying no implementation). Most of what it raised is a delta against
requirements this branch never had; those are listed under "Deltas against the
unmerged RFC revision" below rather than fixed here. One item was a defect
under the RFC this branch was written against, and is fixed:

10. **GUDHI's `extended_persistence()` 4-tuple got a row-shape error.** It
    reached `_columns_from_pairs` and came back as `row 0 of a persistence()
    list must be (dim, (birth, death)); got [(0, (0.0, 1.0))]` — true, and it
    sends a caller hunting for a typo in data that is exactly what GUDHI
    returned. §11's table lists two GUDHI forms and this is neither, so
    `_is_extended_persistence` now refuses the tuple by name, before any
    per-row work. `TypeError`, matching the fallthrough that already refuses
    unaccepted forms; §11 fixes that it is refused, not what it raises.

    Keyed on *tuple of exactly four lists*, not on length: a `persistence()`
    result of four bars is also four things long, and the guard test
    `test_from_gudhi_still_accepts_a_four_row_persistence_list` is what stops
    the check widening onto real input.

    The residual case is documented rather than closed, as §1 and §11 of the
    revised RFC require and as is true regardless of it: **a single member of
    the 4-tuple, passed alone, is undetectable.** The relative and extended-
    members raise on I6 with an error about death times rather than scope; the
    ordinary and extended+ members construct cleanly into a diagram nothing
    here can mark as extended. `from_gudhi`'s docstring says so and tells
    callers not to route extended persistence through it one sub-diagram at a
    time.

Test written first and watched fail for the right reason (the misleading
row-shape `ValueError`). `299 passed`, adapter suite at 100% statement and
branch coverage, ruff / ruff format / mypy clean.

Two review items were checked and rejected. The D18 namespace fallback is a
recorded deferral, not an oversight — `_namespace_of`'s docstring explains that
adding it here alone yields diagrams whose namespace `core.py` cannot
re-derive, and `TODO.md` carries it; acting on it in this branch would be
wrong. And the tests asserting that `from_ripser` and `from_persim` accept
plain nested Python rows were called a conflict with "reject unlisted input
forms"; that clause names `from_gudhi` as where it bites and is about the
extended-persistence tuple, not about container types. The support is
deliberate — a caller round-tripping a fixture through JSON hands over lists —
and stays, as a documented extension rather than a claim about §11.
